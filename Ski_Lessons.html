Club 1 — Days 1–3

Foundations: What We Learned

Standing on Slope: Static Edge Hold

Feet parallel to the fall line, carving with the metal edge to hold position. Knees bent, weight and centre of gravity over the lower leg.

This is the base pattern that everything else builds on.

Weight Distribution Principle

Weight on the downhill leg — the fall side. This is counterintuitive because the instinct is to lean into the slope for safety, and the whole skill development is about overriding that instinct.

The biomechanically correct position feels "wrong" but is correct technique for control.

Dorsiflexion & Knee Position

Ankles in dorsiflexion, knees pressed forward. This maintains balance, keeps you over your skis, and gives you control authority.

Not a passive position — an actively held one.

Turn the Skis, Not the Body

The injury-prevention principle. If the body rotates but the skis don't follow, the torsion goes through the ligaments. Motion initiates from the hips, not the knees — the knees are stabilisers, not drivers.

Ski Pole Technique

Hold poles relaxed at the front as default position. When turning, shift upper body weight and tilt poles toward the downhill leg. Keep body straight — pivot to turn but never twist the upper body. Poles should angle behind you while skiing, for safety.

Contributed by classmate notes.

Technique Progression
Wedge snowplow
Wedge turns + parallel traverse
Letting the skis naturally come parallel during traversal
"Butter the toast" — spread the wedge to initiate turns, then slide back to parallel after the turn
Navigate turns and longer S-curves using that same mechanic
The Wave Course

Dorsiflexion, bent knees, flexible not rigid — the suspension system metaphor. Responsiveness over stiffness.

Overarching Philosophy

Control speed, control turns, build muscle memory. Work with your skis and the terrain rather than fighting them. Trust the equipment to move into position. Feel how stable and controlled it is when executed correctly.

Club 2 — Day 4

Refining the Technique

Heel-Initiated Turns

Turning should initiate with the heels, with toes held in dorsiflexion. This puts you in proper alignment to activate the right motion.

Dorsiflexion is no longer just for balance — it's the initiation point for turning.

Don't Lead with Shoulders

The lower body does the work, the shoulders stay quiet. Same principle as "turn your skis, not your body" — now with finer precision.

Weight Distribution Self-Test

Weight on the downhill leg means the uphill leg is light and can easily be lifted. If you can't lift the uphill ski easily, your weight distribution needs adjusting.

A real-time check you can run on yourself, on the slope.

Stance Equivalence

The relaxed stance standing with skis perpendicular to the fall line is the same feeling you want while traversing. Don't search for a new sensation in motion — reproduce a feeling you already know.

Skiing on Loose Snow Pebbles

On compacted balls of loose snow, keep skis close together and parallel for control. A wedge risks catching and spreading wider. Parallel is more stable in choppy terrain.

Don't Panic

Lean into the motion, forward. Pulling back reduces control over the skis. Stay loose, agile, flexible rather than bracing.

At this stage, the technique is understood. The challenge is trusting the body to execute what the mind already knows.

Glide. Enjoy the flow.
